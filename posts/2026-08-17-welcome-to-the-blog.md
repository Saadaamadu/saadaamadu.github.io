I wrote most of this a year ago, during a summer in Malawi, and I'm only now getting around to hitting publish — but it still feels like the right way to start. Welcome to the blog!

I love all things food: I love eating food, I love cooking food, I love growing food, I love talking about food, I love looking at and buying food, I love researching food — I just love food. I'm an urbanite and an urbanist through and through.

I was born and raised in Tamale, a city of about half a million people in Northern Ghana. I didn't grow up growing food myself — my earliest experience of having direct access to my food source was my grandmother's mango trees. The neighborhood I grew up in was close to a fairly big forest, which has sadly since been torn down; I only realized that when I went back to Ghana in 2023 looking for those mango trees. That's a story for another day.

As a child, mango season was the highlight of my year. We'd harvest mangoes and sell them in front of the compound house I grew up in, and I remember getting sick almost every season from eating too many. Those were happy times. My childhood in Tamale was very attached to the seasons — there was also a popular insect season that coincided with the rains, when insects were in abundance and we'd "harvest" and roast them for eating. It's been about two decades since I last climbed my grandmother's mango trees or ate those insects.

I am an extremely proud African, born and raised in Ghana, and my values, experiences, and aspirations are shaped by the continent. Africa is top of mind in my research interests, too.

I've had the privilege of traveling to many cities in Asia and Europe, but far fewer in Africa — and my main travel goal for the rest of my PhD is to change that. My dissertation is shaped around food systems resilience in West Africa, so I already have plans to visit more of the sub-region, and I'm looking forward to exploring more of Southern Africa after Zimbabwe and, currently, Malawi. I'm hoping to add a couple of cities in East Africa before the end of the year.

I love to cook — everything from frying an egg to dishes that take days of prep. I recently watched a clip of Edison Chen on *Subway Takes* where he says "he doesn't cook for people he doesn't love," and that really resonated with me. I didn't like cooking much as a kid or teenager, despite growing up in a culture that put a lot of emphasis on young women learning to cook — my stepmom tried for years to teach me, and I mostly resisted, though I'd still sit in the kitchen while she spent hours making sure there were dishes in the fridge for the family. It wasn't until I moved out that I realized some of it had stuck after all. Between cookbooks, videos, recipe sites, and the occasional class, I've kept learning since. I love cooking for friends, though between a PhD program and everything else, I don't get to host as often as I'd like.

Some travel highlights so far: Cairo, Chefchaouen, Hong Kong, Kota Kinabalu, Budapest, San Sebastián, Austin, New York, and Cartagena. I love cities, and I think they're going to stay under enormous pressure as more people move into them — which is a lot of what pulls me toward my research: how cities build resilience into the provision of things like food and health as their populations grow.

## What this blog is for

I'm currently a PhD student in City and Regional Planning at UC Berkeley, with research interests at the intersection of agriculture, food, climate, economic development, resilience, and cities. In practical terms, that means I spend a lot of time thinking about:

- How smallholder farmers across West Africa, and the continent more broadly, are adapting to climate change and other shocks and stressors — economic crises, conflict, health crises — to stay resilient
- How capital gets deployed to smallholder farmers and food producers in the face of those shocks, and what that means for the resilience of their livelihoods

This blog will follow two loose formats: informal posts on food, Africa, and cities — travel, restaurants, cooking, general appreciation — and a more research-heavy format on the same three topics. Right now that second kind of post looks like trying to understand the impact of the USAID funding cuts on Malawi's agrifood sector, which I've been asking people about directly while working here this summer.

I'm still an early-stage PhD student and my research interests are broad — part of the hope for this blog is to use it to narrow things down. Thanks for reading, and welcome.
